- 2026-10-12, **Low-Cost Deep Learning for Automated Keyframe Interpolation in Sprite Animation**, Kenneth Alonso et.al., Paper: [@2610.16423](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16423)
> **Abstract:** Sprite-based animation remains prevalent in video game development, but manual in-betweening---generating intermediate frames between keyframes---is labor-intensive. Existing video frame interpolation methods, optimized for photorealistic content, introduce smoothing artifacts when applied to the sharp edges, limited color palettes, and stylized motion characteristic of hand-drawn sprites. We present a deep learning approach for sprite in-betweening that adapts a U-Net encoder-decoder architecture with domain-specific modifications, including RGBA-aware processing, multi-scale flow refinement, and alpha-gated blending to preserve pixel-perfect sprite aesthetics. Following data-centric AI principles, we demonstrate that a few carefully curated sprite animation triplets---selected for diversity, quality, and representative edge cases---enable effective training without requiring massive generic datasets. Experimental evaluation using the FLIP perceptual metric shows that our method achieves a mean error of 0.258 on 42 test triplets, outperforming state-of-the-art video interpolation RIFE (0.313, 17.5\% worse), traditional optical flow Lucas-Kanade (0.449, 42.6\% worse), and general frame synthesis CyclicGen (0.603, 57.2\% worse). This study shows that quality-over-quantity data curation and domain-specific architectural adaptations yield better interpolation quality than general-purpose methods trained on orders of magnitude more data, validating the data-centric AI paradigm for specialized visual domains. This is particularly important for small-to-medium-sized game development studios, where budget and time constraints are critical.

- 2026-10-19, **ExMR: Extremity Motion Refinement for Monocular Human Motion Capture**, Tom Wehrbein et.al., Paper: [@2610.26213](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26213)
> **Abstract:** Detailed hand and foot articulation is important for animation and motion analysis, yet remains challenging to recover from monocular video. Recent expressive video methods infer hand articulation from shared whole-body representations. Our conference work suggests that this underexploits local evidence: the Foot Motion Refinement (FootMR) module refines ankle rotations from foot keypoints, improving reconstruction even when the initial estimator receives the same keypoints. We extend this principle to hands with the Hand Motion Refinement (HandMR) module. Because 2D finger keypoints are often missing or unreliable, HandMR combines image features of a pretrained hand reconstruction model with initial arm and hand rotations to refine elbows, wrists, and fingers. We integrate FootMR and HandMR into ExMR (Extremity Motion Refinement), a system for reconstructing 3D body, hand, and foot motion from monocular video. An initial temporal estimator predicts whole-body motion, after which both refiners revisit local evidence to predict residual joint rotations. Experiments on ARCTIC, MOYO, RICH, and MOOF show that ExMR outperforms state-of-the-art methods in hand and foot reconstruction, reducing hand per-vertex error and ankle joint angle error on MOYO by 19% and 18%, respectively, relative to the strongest competitor. Code will be made available for research purposes. Project page: https://twehrbein.github.io/exmr-website/

> **Abstract:** Long-horizon human-scene interaction requires coordinated spatial reasoning, physical feasibility, and the ability to recover from compounding execution errors. - 2026-10-17, **MOMO: Human Motion from Moving Sounds**, Deli Cai et.al., Paper: [@2610.24522](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24522)
> **Abstract:** Human responses to sound depend not only on its semantic content, but also on the evolving spatial relationship between source and listener, conveyed through spatial audio and its temporal dynamics. Experiments show that MOMO outperforms existing baselines in motion fidelity, audio-motion alignment, and physical plausibility.
- 2026-10-16, **ULIFLOW: Unified Latent Human-Object Interaction Flow**, Wenqi Cai et.al., Paper: [@2610.24286](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24286)
> **Abstract:** Text-conditioned 3D human--object interaction (HOI) generation remains challenging because individually plausible human and object motions do not necessarily form a coherent interaction. - 2026-10-15, **Dense4D: Pixel-Level 4D Human Correspondence**, Rıza Alp Güler et.al., Paper: [@2610.22325](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22325)
> **Abstract:** Reconstructing human motion in 4D requires identifying the same surface points through articulation, deformation and changes in visibility. We introduce Dense4D, to our knowledge the first feed-forward model dedicated to dense 4D correspondence across the body, clothing and hair from a set of monocular RGB images, without a body template. Trained specifically for human geometry, Dense4D jointly processes person-centred crops with known image-to-crop transforms to predict Human Point Maps (HPMs) and depth for each image. An HPM assigns each person pixel the 3D position of its observed surface point at a common reference time. Experiments on real people demonstrate substantially higher tracking accuracy and closer agreement with scanned surfaces than the evaluated baselines. Points from different frames assemble into coherent human geometry in the reference pose, providing visible evidence of consistent dense correspondence through articulation and clothing deformation. Additional qualitative results, videos, and an interactive viewer are available at https://alpguler.com/dense4d.
