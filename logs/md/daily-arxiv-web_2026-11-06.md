## Motion Authoring, Editing & Retargeting

- 2026-11-05, **REACT: Towards Real-Time Completion of Skeletal Motion Signals**, P. de Clermont Gallerande et.al., Paper: [@2611.08301](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08301)
> **Abstract:** Tracking user motion in interactive and immersive applications is increasingly common (e.g., video games, XR systems, smartphones). Yet, processing and transmitting human motion data remain critical challenges, as current approaches strongly rely on the quality of the tracked motion signals. In general, affordable motion-capture systems may produce unnatural animations in the presence of calibration errors, occlusions, sensor drift, high-frequency noise, or other artifacts. More specifically, we simulate occlusions and high-frequency noise and propose to mitigate their effects with REACT, an interactive online motion-completion framework operating causally, frame by frame, without look-ahead. REACT is an S4-based autoregressive model that coordinates complementary motion estimates under specific reliability-dependent constraints and exposes several interpretable internal signals. We present a system-level contribution consisting of a structured formulation of interactive motion completion together with a coherent instantiation of this formulation. We evaluate REACT against several causal baselines and architectural variants under varying levels of occlusion severity, and report their respective inference latencies. Our results show that REACT achieves a relevant compromise between reconstruction accuracy, update smoothness, and interactive inference. Finally, we characterize how its fusion behaviour changes under missing input observations.

> **Abstract:** Modern video models possess strong visual priors, yet do not natively represent human action in metric 3D space. Despite adaptation only on controlled laboratory recordings, EgoGeneralist generalizes to diverse unseen scenes and interactions with effective action control and visual quality, bridging pretrained visual knowledge and metric 3D human motion.

- 2026-11-04, **Semantic Residual Vector Quantization for Sign Language Generation**, Chang Liu et.al., Paper: [@2611.06781](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06781)
> **Abstract:** Sign language generation (SLG) maps written sentences into signing motion while preserving both lexical content and fine-grained articulation. This requires a model to decide what signs should be expressed and to realize them through precise handshape, trajectory, facial expression and body coordination. Existing pose-based systems and reconstruction-only motion tokenizers often entangle these two decisions, leaving the generator without an explicit text-aligned intermediate target for what to sign before refining how to sign it. To address this issue, we introduce Semantic Residual Vector Quantization (SRVQ), a framework for SLG that frames generation as a two-level process moving from semantics to kinematics. We evaluate using retrieval and FID in a shared text-motion embedding space, alongside standard geometric metrics. On CSL-Daily and Phoenix-2014T, SRVQ achieves R@1 of 82.3 and 70.3, outperforming the previous SOTA by 30 and 46 points, respectively.
- 2026-11-04, **Human Motion Prediction via Continual Prior Compensation**, Jianwei Tang et.al., Paper: [@2611.06637](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06637)
> **Abstract:** Human Motion Prediction (HMP) aims to predict future human poses at different moments according to observed past motion sequences. Previous approaches mainly treated the prediction of different temporal moments as a single prediction task and learned the predictions of varied moments simultaneously, which would encounter a main limitation: the learning of short-term predictions (referring to ``near-future'' prediction) could be hindered by the predictions of long-term (referring to ``far-future'' prediction) motions. In this paper, we develop a novel temporal continual learning framework called Continual Prior Compensation (CPC) to progressively train HMP models, in which we divide the prediction task of motions corresponding to varied temporal moments into several subtasks and train the model in a multi-stage manner. To mitigate the prior information forgetting in the progressive training, we further introduce a learnable random variable Prior Compensation Factor (PCF) to explicitly measure the prior knowledge loss. We theoretically show that the PCF can be efficiently learned together with the model parameters by minimizing a reasonable upper bound of the objective function. The proposed CPC is further enhanced to estimate the prior information loss for each subtask and a new framework called Continual Prior Compensation++ (CPC++) with Fine-Grained Prior Compensation Factor (FGPCF) is finally developed. Our CPC and CPC++ frameworks are quite flexible and can be easily integrated with different HMP backbone models and adapted to various datasets and applications. Extensive experiments on three HMP benchmark datasets using multiple SOTA HMP backbones (PGBIG, siMLPe, MotionMixer, and LTD) demonstrate the effectiveness and flexibility of our frameworks.

> **Abstract:** Motion language models (MoLMs) encode 3D human motion as discrete tokens for language-based understanding. Deploying them on monocular video raises two problems. - 2026-10-28, **StepControl: Long-Horizon Human Motion Generation from Foot-Contact Schedules**, Hojun Jang et.al., Paper: [@2610.38153](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38153)
> **Abstract:** Controllable human motion generation has largely focused on specifying what action should occur or where the body should move. We study a complementary question: can full-body motion be controlled by specifying only when each foot contacts the ground? We introduce StepControl, an autoregressive diffusion model conditioned on a future binary left/right foot-contact schedule, without requiring target root and body poses, or semantic prompts. - 2026-11-06, **On Agentic Manipulation Policies**, Chen Chen et.al., Paper: [@2611.09892](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09892)
> **Abstract:** Recent work on robot manipulation has focused on specialist policies trained on demonstrations. We show that generalist AI models can control manipulators more reliably than specialist policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs). We systematically analyze agentic manipulation policies (AMPs), in which a generalist AI model commands the manipulator directly, adapting its actions as the task unfolds. We present AMP1, a reference harness that runs unchanged across single-arm, bimanual, and mobile manipulation. AMP1 achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboLab-120 and CaP-Gym benchmarks, with no demonstrations or task-specific training. On RoboLab-120, AMP1 reaches an 85.6\% success rate zero-shot, compared to 49.2\% and 42.9\% for leading VLA and WAM policies. The basic harness supplies no grasping, pushing, or placing skills; the agent develops its own strategies, such as searching for hidden objects, using objects as tools, and calibrating its cameras. With the addition of a persistent memory store, the agent can also learn from experience: an augmented harness with a memory representation that is carried across episodes lowers the failure rate on RoboDojo bimanual manipulation tasks by 4.6x relative to zero-shot deployment. Our analysis suggests that AMPs are substantially more general and robust than specialist manipulation policies, providing a promising foundation for robot manipulation.

> **Abstract:** 3D spatial reasoning from video remains challenging for VLMs because videos provide sequential, view-local observations rather than persistent metric scene records. > **Abstract:** Vision-language-action (VLA) models typically generate actions in chunks, organizing continuous robot execution into separately predicted segments and thus introducing potential discontinuities at action-chunk transitions. > **Abstract:** Real-world audio applications require precise, queryable representations grounded in physical time.
