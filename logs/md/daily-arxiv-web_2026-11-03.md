## Motion Authoring, Editing & Retargeting

- 2026-10-28, **Decompose, Don't Expand: Capability-Independent AI Systems by Construction**, Tejaswi Gowda et.al., Paper: [@2610.38144](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38144)
> **Abstract:** A recurring design choice in building AI systems is whether, when a task is too hard for a model, to expand the model (make it larger, better-prompted, fine-tuned), or to decompose the task (extract the parts that admit a deterministic solution to a host, leaving the model only the irreducibly fuzzy residue). We make three claims. First, that many tasks commonly delegated whole to a model are in fact deterministic tasks with a small generative core. Second, and our central empirical result, that decomposing such a task yields a measurable and desirable property we call parity by construction: on the extracted sub-tasks the model-size performance curve becomes flat, because no model performs them at all. We measure this across nine models spanning a 494M-parameter local checkpoint to a frontier Opus-class model, a thirty-fold parameter range on the local family alone: once the host resolves the context, operation selection scores identically for all nine models and selector resolution for eight of nine, while the one task that does not decompose, labelling, remains sloped across the same range. Because the task leaves the model, a stock checkpoint suffices: no task-specific training and no fine-tuning. Third, that this is a general design discipline, not a domain trick: we show the same move recurring across physics setup, motion retargeting, and object-model editing, and in a Web-CLI tool family (same author, so not an independent replication). We give a method for locating the cut, the construction that makes a cut exist (a deterministic surface and a closed grammar over it), and a way to make the residue safe (resolve the deterministic context; verify the generative output), position the work against neuro-symbolic AI, tool-augmented language models, and generate-and-verify approaches, and are explicit about what is measured (one domain) versus illustrative (the rest).

- 2026-11-03, **Möbius: A Unified Motion-Language Model Bridging Understanding and Generation**, Jiazhi Shu et.al., Paper: [@2611.05279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05279)
> **Abstract:** We aim to build a truly unified motion model that can understand, generate, and edit human motion within a single framework. Existing motion UMMs remain only partially unified: motion understanding is handled by a language model, while generation is often delegated to a separate diffusion module operating over learned motion latents. We introduce Möbius, which brings the entire diffusion process into the same multimodal representation space used for motion understanding. Clean motion, noisy motion, and text jointly participate in shared attention, while role-specific parameters process understanding and generation streams. Importantly, Möbius operates directly on continuous frame-level raw motion, requiring neither discrete tokenization nor a pretrained motion VAE. This simple formulation naturally supports motion captioning, text-to-motion generation, temporal completion, and instruction-guided editing within a single model. On HumanML3D, Möbius achieves state-of-the-art performance among unified motion models. Scaling to 1.7B parameters and 1,700 hours of motion data further demonstrates that our framework is both effective and scalable.
- 2026-11-01, **MoPort: A View-Aware 2D Interface for Motion Language Models**, Lixing Tan et.al., Paper: [@2611.01541](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01541)
> **Abstract:** Motion language models (MoLMs) encode 3D human motion as discrete tokens for language-based understanding. Deploying them on monocular video raises two problems. Pose detection yields 2D joint sequences, leaving a dimensional gap from the 3D motion used in training. Moreover, the 2D observation changes with the camera viewpoint, while the depth lost in projection makes the 2D pose ambiguous across viewpoints. We propose MoPort, a view-aware 2D interface that enables MoLMs to take 2D observations as input. We introduce Paired View Alignment (PVA), which gives two views of the same motion a shared 3D teacher target and directly constrains the consistency of their latents, so that 2D observations from different viewpoints are mapped to a consistent semantic representation. Camera-Modulated Encoding (CME) estimates the camera extrinsics from the 2D sequence and uses them to modulate encoding, providing the 2D latents with geometric information about the viewpoint. MoPort achieves R@1 of 0.5674 on HumanML3D and 0.3286 on KIT-ML, outperforming all evaluated baselines. On HumanML3D, its R@1 decreases only from 0.5763 on in-distribution viewpoints to 0.5585 on out-of-distribution viewpoints. We validate portability on three downstream MoLMs. Furthermore, on the real-world RGB video dataset Fit3D, MoPort achieves 80.36\% accuracy. Code will be released upon acceptance.

- 2026-10-23, **Past as State, Present as Attention: Persistent-State Blockwise Flow Matching for Long-Horizon Co-Speech Motion Generation**, Yi Liu et.al., Paper: [@2610.32373](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32373)
> **Abstract:** Co-speech motion generation remains challenging over long horizons due to the need for temporal coherence and consistent style. > **Abstract:** Body deformation caused by muscle contraction plays a vital role in the visual realism of character animation, yet in most 3D animation pipelines artists have to manually specify muscle activations, which is time-consuming and gives no guarantee of physical realism. > **Abstract:** Inverse kinematics (IK) converts end-effector pose commands into joint motion, and many vision-language-action (VLA) policies rely on it to act on a given arm and to transfer across embodiments. These results show that reinforcement learning can provide high-precision inverse kinematics for robot arms. Code is available at https://github.com/Tinaliu0123/PolicyIK.

- 2026-11-03, **Should the Action Expert Train the VLM? Action-Token Supervision and Gradient Routing in Mixture-of-Transformer VLAs**, Asher James Hancock et.al., Paper: [@2611.05249](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05249)
> **Abstract:** Vision-language-action (VLA) models increasingly pair a pre-trained vision-language model (VLM) with a flow-matching expert that conditions its action predictions on the VLM's key-value representations. Those representations must carry information useful for control, yet the VLM was never trained for the action expert's flow-matching objective. Two signals can address this: gradients backpropagated from the expert's loss (coupling), or VLM supervision to predict discrete tokens encoding the same target actions. Prominent training recipes forgo the first, blocking expert-to-VLM gradients (insulation) to protect the VLM's existing capabilities, while also supervising it on vision-language data and action tokens. These findings show that the value of gradient insulation depends on the VLM's action supervision and the evaluation setting, challenging insulation as a default VLA training recipe.
