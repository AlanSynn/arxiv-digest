## Motion Authoring, Editing & Retargeting

- 2026-10-28, **Decompose, Don't Expand: Capability-Independent AI Systems by Construction**, Tejaswi Gowda et.al., Paper: [@2610.38144](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38144)
> **Abstract:** A recurring design choice in building AI systems is whether, when a task is too hard for a model, to expand the model (make it larger, better-prompted, fine-tuned), or to decompose the task (extract the parts that admit a deterministic solution to a host, leaving the model only the irreducibly fuzzy residue). We make three claims. First, that many tasks commonly delegated whole to a model are in fact deterministic tasks with a small generative core. Second, and our central empirical result, that decomposing such a task yields a measurable and desirable property we call parity by construction: on the extracted sub-tasks the model-size performance curve becomes flat, because no model performs them at all. We measure this across nine models spanning a 494M-parameter local checkpoint to a frontier Opus-class model, a thirty-fold parameter range on the local family alone: once the host resolves the context, operation selection scores identically for all nine models and selector resolution for eight of nine, while the one task that does not decompose, labelling, remains sloped across the same range. Because the task leaves the model, a stock checkpoint suffices: no task-specific training and no fine-tuning. Third, that this is a general design discipline, not a domain trick: we show the same move recurring across physics setup, motion retargeting, and object-model editing, and in a Web-CLI tool family (same author, so not an independent replication). We give a method for locating the cut, the construction that makes a cut exist (a deterministic surface and a closed grammar over it), and a way to make the residue safe (resolve the deterministic context; verify the generative output), position the work against neuro-symbolic AI, tool-augmented language models, and generate-and-verify approaches, and are explicit about what is measured (one domain) versus illustrative (the rest).

## Character Motion Synthesis & Animation

- 2026-10-30, **Movens-Zero: Scaling Humanoid Motion Foundation Models with Large-Scale Human Videos**, Wendong Bu et.al., Paper: [@2610.41877](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41877)
> **Abstract:** Scaling humanoid motion foundation models is constrained by the scarcity of diverse, robot-executable data and by the difficulty of jointly learning from heterogeneous multimodal inputs while supporting real-time closed-loop control. We present Movens-5M, constructed from raw Internet videos through clip generation, quality filtering, semantic annotation, motion recovery, and physical refinement with humanoid retargeting and execution verification. It contains 15 million human-motion sequences and 5 million physically executable humanoid-motion sequences spanning over 10,000 hours, aligned with text, RGB video, speech, and music. Building on this data, we introduce Movens-Zero, a scalable understanding-generation-execution architecture that supports joint learning across modalities and real-time closed-loop execution. Task-mixed flow-matching training jointly optimizes a single action expert across seven generation tasks, enabling direct motion generation from text, speech, music, and video. Multimodal Real-Time Chunking connects overlapping chunks under continuously updated instructions and robot states for continuous closed-loop execution. We further introduce Movens-Intent, a four-track benchmark with 8,192 expert-verified condition-motion pairs across text, speech, music, and video. Experiments show consistent gains across all four modalities when scaling execution-verified data from 1% to 100% and action-expert capacity from 64M to 911M parameters, while demonstrating that data quality and cross-modal supervision improve generation and execution.
- 2026-10-29, **TalkFlow: Structured Latent Flow Matching for Speech Driven Holistic Motion Generation**, Xinmu Wang et.al., Paper: [@2610.40972](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40972)
> **Abstract:** Holistic co-speech motion generation aims to synthesize coordinated facial expressions, hand gestures, and body movements from speech, which is challenging due to heterogeneous part-specific dynamics, long-range temporal coherence, and cross-part coordination. Directly modeling high-dimensional motion can be unstable, while overly compact or unstructured latent spaces may lose fine-grained motion details. We present \textbf{TalkFlow}, a structured latent flow framework for speech-driven holistic motion generation. TalkFlow learns a continuous three-stream latent representation for upper-body motion, lower-body motion, and facial expressions, preserving part-specific dynamics without forcing heterogeneous motion components into a single latent space. To maintain holistic coordination, lightweight cross-stream interactions are incorporated in both latent representation learning and flow-based generation. Conditioned on multi-level speech features, TalkFlow performs speech-conditioned latent flow matching, learning a continuous transport process from source latents to realistic motion latents. Experiments on the BEAT2 dataset demonstrate strong performance across facial expression accuracy, body gesture quality, and speech-motion synchronization. Qualitative comparisons and user studies further show that TalkFlow generates more natural, expressive, and speech-aligned holistic motion than state-of-the-art methods. The project page is available at https://xinmuwang.github.io/TalkFlow/.
- 2026-10-28, **ETA-Motion: Efficient Topology-Agnostic Motion Diffusion via Sparse Joint Learning**, Seoyoung Hwang et.al., Paper: [@2610.38259](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38259)
> **Abstract:** Topology-agnostic motion generation aims to synthesize motions for diverse characters with arbitrary skeletons. Unlike conventional motion generation, which assumes a fixed skeletal template, this setting must handle diverse joint configurations and inconsistent joint semantics across characters. Existing methods address this challenge through dense full-joint modeling, which incurs substantial computational cost and hinders learning transferable motion patterns across diverse skeletal topologies. We present ETA-Motion, a topology-agnostic motion generation framework based on sparse joint learning. Instead of learning motion from all joints, our method uses a compact subset of joints to reduce redundancy and trains the motion generator on the resulting sparse skeleton. Although ETA-Motion is trained only on sparse skeletal observations, it supports full-skeleton motion generation during inference. Experimental results demonstrate that ETA-Motion outperforms existing topology-agnostic baselines while significantly improving efficiency and generalization to unseen motions and unseen topologies.
- 2026-10-28, **StepControl: Long-Horizon Human Motion Generation from Foot-Contact Schedules**, Hojun Jang et.al., Paper: [@2610.38153](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38153)
> **Abstract:** Controllable human motion generation has largely focused on specifying what action should occur or where the body should move. We study a complementary question: can full-body motion be controlled by specifying only when each foot contacts the ground? We introduce StepControl, an autoregressive diffusion model conditioned on a future binary left/right foot-contact schedule, without requiring target root and body poses, or semantic prompts. StepControl represents contact as explicit temporal events and injects them through dedicated cross-attention with classifier-free guidance. We further introduce a stochastic mixture noise schedule and warmup strategy for stable long-horizon autoregressive generation. Experiments show that StepControl follows prescribed contact timing more accurately than adapting existing spatially guided motion generators to contact constraints. It maintains effective contact control over long continuous rollouts and responds to edited schedules such as cadence changes, stop-and-go pauses, limping gaits, and bilateral jumping. Our results show that minimal foot-contact schedules provide an effective and intuitive temporal interface for controllable motion generation.

- 2026-10-23, **Past as State, Present as Attention: Persistent-State Blockwise Flow Matching for Long-Horizon Co-Speech Motion Generation**, Yi Liu et.al., Paper: [@2610.32373](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32373)
> **Abstract:** Co-speech motion generation remains challenging over long horizons due to the need for temporal coherence and consistent style. Existing methods typically generate long motions segment by segment, either conditioning on a short seed of recent frames, which gradually drifts, or retaining ever-longer explicit histories, which are costly to maintain and hard to learn from limited data. We trace these limitations to a key asymmetry between past and present: past motion carries slowly varying style that should be compactly summarized, whereas the current segment exhibits fast-changing local dynamics that require fine-grained modeling. This asymmetry motivates a simple Past-As-State, Present-as-Attention principle for long-horizon co-speech motion generation. Following this principle, we propose PASPA, which summarizes past history with an inter-block persistent state and models the current segment with intra-block bidirectional self-attention. We instantiate PASPA within a blockwise flow matching framework, with multi-block supervision for effective training over variable histories, prefill-decode state caching for efficient rollout, and hybrid classifier-free guidance for enhanced history and speech conditioning. Extensive public-benchmark experiments show that PASPA achieves state-of-the-art FGD performance and preserves motion style across long-sequence generation.

- 2026-10-21, **RLMuscle: Muscle-Driven Character Animation via Reinforcement Learning**, Chunlei Li et.al., Paper: [@2610.30033](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30033)
> **Abstract:** Body deformation caused by muscle contraction plays a vital role in the visual realism of character animation, yet in most 3D animation pipelines artists have to manually specify muscle activations, which is time-consuming and gives no guarantee of physical realism. To address this, we propose a new method in which a reinforcement learning (RL) controller discovers per-muscle activations that track reference motions without manual tuning. Existing RL muscle controllers stop at 1D muscles; we go one step further, keeping the 1D model inside the RL control loop as a fast surrogate, and using a 3D volumetric simulation to produce the final deformation. Because both stages share the very same constitutive law, the muscle deformation is produced by the same activations that generated the motion. We demonstrate volumetric muscle simulation on a 194-muscle human body with a diverse set of motions (walk, kick, swim, overhead-squat, etc.), simulating the full-body model at 18 ms per frame on a single RTX 4090. Our work takes a step toward character animation that is not only visually convincing but also biomechanically grounded. The website with code and videos is at https://chunleili.github.io/project-page-rlmuscle/.

> **Abstract:** Foundation models are becoming remarkably reliable at high-level reasoning, yet they remain too slow and costly to serve as fine-grained controllers. Conversely, fast policies like VLAs and whole-body controllers offer more efficient reflexes, but struggle to generalize. While pairing a deliberative brain (foundation models) with a reactive cerebellum (fast policies) seems natural for embodied systems, two obstacles remain: methods are built and scored on incompatible stacks, and their asynchronous coordination is difficult. For example, an agent that calls the VLA in the agentic loop can stall the fast execution, while a VLA that calls the agent on errors tends to re-trigger fallbacks repeatedly or completely miss silent failures. To address this, we propose REA (Routed Embodied Agent), a unified runtime where a deliberative System 2, an embodied router System 1 and reactive System 0 policies cooperate under a single golden rule - no running policy waits on the brain. Instead of querying the brain at each failure, REA employs a route memory that stores each policy's measured success ahead of time, a boundary-triggered judge gate that consults the brain on policy execution, and a shared-budget escalation ladder for bounded recovery. Evaluated on 3,000 test episodes across 60 LIBERO-Pro tasks, REA reaches 95.9% success and beats the best single-agent baseline in its own policy pool (94.7%) while reducing control steps by 25% and control time by 17%. In a separate 100-episode RoboTwin 2.0 study, REA achieves 84% success with VLA policies and 86% with VLA+code, compared with 80% for the strongest single VLA baseline MEDex. These results demonstrate learned and programmatic policies operating within the same runtime. REA also transfers to BEHAVIOR-1K and a real Unitree G1 humanoid, achieving the highest observed success among the compared methods.

- 2026-10-30, **When the Simulator Fails, Not the Policy: Auditing Contact Artifacts in Simulated Robot Benchmarks**, Alex Zhou et.al., Paper: [@2610.41857](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41857)
> **Abstract:** Simulation benchmarks score robot policies by success rate, and every failed episode counts against the policy. Many of these failures are the simulator's fault, not the policy's; we find the cause and fix it. Testing a vision language action model and a world action model in simulation, we find that the policy often places the object and the simulator then ejects it, and that the benchmark's own scripted demonstrations fail the same way. We present SimuGuard, an auditing plugin for LIBERO, RoboTwin, RoboCasa and RoboDojo. It leaves the evaluation unchanged. At every physics step, it records the state and flags an object that starts moving at a contact faster than the robot's contact or gravity account for. It replays a recorded episode bit for bit, and it reruns the episode with one simulation parameter changed. With it we trace these episodes on RoboTwin to the collision detection of the physics engine: for geometry one to two centimeters apart, the engine reports a penetration of about two centimeters, and the solver throws the object out at several meters per second. The cause is one constant: the distance algorithm discards triangles below a fixed area threshold, which valid triangles fall below at the centimeter scale. With that constant changed and nothing else, episodes with an artifact fall from 12 in 100 to none, and the success rate on five container tasks rises from 89% to 98%. Success rates in simulation thus count failures that the simulator causes. Benchmarks on PhysX 5.3.1 or earlier should rerun in a repaired engine. Where the engine cannot change, they should report the success rate together with the range it spans when every flagged episode fails or passes: 75.8% to 98.5% around a reported 83.1% for LingBot-VA.
