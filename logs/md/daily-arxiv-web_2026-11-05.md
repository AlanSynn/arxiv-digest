## Motion Authoring, Editing & Retargeting

- 2026-11-05, **REACT: Towards Real-Time Completion of Skeletal Motion Signals**, P. de Clermont Gallerande et.al., Paper: [@2611.08301](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08301)
> **Abstract:** Tracking user motion in interactive and immersive applications is increasingly common (e.g., video games, XR systems, smartphones). Yet, processing and transmitting human motion data remain critical challenges, as current approaches strongly rely on the quality of the tracked motion signals. In general, affordable motion-capture systems may produce unnatural animations in the presence of calibration errors, occlusions, sensor drift, high-frequency noise, or other artifacts. More specifically, we simulate occlusions and high-frequency noise and propose to mitigate their effects with REACT, an interactive online motion-completion framework operating causally, frame by frame, without look-ahead. REACT is an S4-based autoregressive model that coordinates complementary motion estimates under specific reliability-dependent constraints and exposes several interpretable internal signals. We present a system-level contribution consisting of a structured formulation of interactive motion completion together with a coherent instantiation of this formulation. We evaluate REACT against several causal baselines and architectural variants under varying levels of occlusion severity, and report their respective inference latencies. Our results show that REACT achieves a relevant compromise between reconstruction accuracy, update smoothness, and interactive inference. Finally, we characterize how its fusion behaviour changes under missing input observations.

> **Abstract:** Modern video models possess strong visual priors, yet do not natively represent human action in metric 3D space. - 2026-11-04, **Semantic Residual Vector Quantization for Sign Language Generation**, Chang Liu et.al., Paper: [@2611.06781](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06781)
> **Abstract:** Sign language generation (SLG) maps written sentences into signing motion while preserving both lexical content and fine-grained articulation. This requires a model to decide what signs should be expressed and to realize them through precise handshape, trajectory, facial expression and body coordination. Existing pose-based systems and reconstruction-only motion tokenizers often entangle these two decisions, leaving the generator without an explicit text-aligned intermediate target for what to sign before refining how to sign it. To address this issue, we introduce Semantic Residual Vector Quantization (SRVQ), a framework for SLG that frames generation as a two-level process moving from semantics to kinematics. On CSL-Daily and Phoenix-2014T, SRVQ achieves R@1 of 82.3 and 70.3, outperforming the previous SOTA by 30 and 46 points, respectively.
- 2026-11-04, **Human Motion Prediction via Continual Prior Compensation**, Jianwei Tang et.al., Paper: [@2611.06637](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06637)
> **Abstract:** Human Motion Prediction (HMP) aims to predict future human poses at different moments according to observed past motion sequences. Previous approaches mainly treated the prediction of different temporal moments as a single prediction task and learned the predictions of varied moments simultaneously, which would encounter a main limitation: the learning of short-term predictions (referring to ``near-future'' prediction) could be hindered by the predictions of long-term (referring to ``far-future'' prediction) motions. In this paper, we develop a novel temporal continual learning framework called Continual Prior Compensation (CPC) to progressively train HMP models, in which we divide the prediction task of motions corresponding to varied temporal moments into several subtasks and train the model in a multi-stage manner. To mitigate the prior information forgetting in the progressive training, we further introduce a learnable random variable Prior Compensation Factor (PCF) to explicitly measure the prior knowledge loss. We theoretically show that the PCF can be efficiently learned together with the model parameters by minimizing a reasonable upper bound of the objective function. The proposed CPC is further enhanced to estimate the prior information loss for each subtask and a new framework called Continual Prior Compensation++ (CPC++) with Fine-Grained Prior Compensation Factor (FGPCF) is finally developed. Our CPC and CPC++ frameworks are quite flexible and can be easily integrated with different HMP backbone models and adapted to various datasets and applications. Extensive experiments on three HMP benchmark datasets using multiple SOTA HMP backbones (PGBIG, siMLPe, MotionMixer, and LTD) demonstrate the effectiveness and flexibility of our frameworks.

- 2026-11-01, **MoPort: A View-Aware 2D Interface for Motion Language Models**, Lixing Tan et.al., Paper: [@2611.01541](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01541)
> **Abstract:** Motion language models (MoLMs) encode 3D human motion as discrete tokens for language-based understanding. Deploying them on monocular video raises two problems. - 2026-10-23, **Past as State, Present as Attention: Persistent-State Blockwise Flow Matching for Long-Horizon Co-Speech Motion Generation**, Yi Liu et.al., Paper: [@2610.32373](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32373)

## Vision-Language & Action Models (VLA/VLM)

- 2026-11-05, **OopsieRecover: Learning Transferable VLA Corrections from Semantic Interventions**, Arpit Bahety et.al., Paper: [@2611.08462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08462)
> **Abstract:** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform a wide range of robotic manipulation tasks, but often fail during deployment. For instance, DAgger-like fine-tuning can require tens to hundreds of interactions, yet the resulting policy may still fail to generalize the learned corrections beyond the demonstrated scenarios. These costs capture when a behavior is undesirable and steer the policy toward more desirable action regions. We experiment across 6 tasks and variations in object poses, scenes, and related tasks, and find that only a handful of corrective demonstrations can enable a VLA to overcome failures across a broad range of novel situations, without requiring new corrective data for each variation. Please refer to https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/oopsierecover for videos.

- 2026-11-05, **Seeing Beyond the Next Step: World-Model-Guided Human-Like Navigation in Multi-Agent Scenes**, Seong-Eun Hong et.al., Paper: [@2611.07730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07730)
> **Abstract:** Selecting a next motion in a populated scene is not just a matter of avoiding what is currently in the way: an action can be locally feasible yet drift onto the road instead of the crosswalk, cut into a stream of crossing pedestrians, or bottleneck a narrow passage. These failure modes are invisible from the current state alone, and acting on them requires reasoning about what each candidate action will cause. World models offer exactly this, namely action-conditioned predictions of the future, but rolling them out at every decision step is a cost deployable navigation systems cannot pay, and the field has largely traded foresight for speed. We argue this is a false dichotomy. At deployment, both world models are removed and the policy acts in a single forward pass. Across five held-out evaluation scenarios, this yields navigation that is simultaneously safer, more socially and group-coherent, and more goal-directed than strong reactive and learned baselines, with naive observers rating the resulting motion as more human-like. Taken together, our findings point to a different approach for bringing world-model foresight into deployable agents: not accelerating rollouts, but amortizing them into the policy so that the cost of looking ahead is confined to training. > **Abstract:** Real-world audio applications require precise, queryable representations grounded in physical time. Yet modern Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) increasingly favor conversational generation, producing free-form descriptions that conflate acoustic perception with higher-level reasoning and lack precise temporal grounding. We introduce TAC2, a foundation model that casts timestamped audio captioning as composable functions to construct granular, speaker-attributed acoustic timelines directly from in-the-wild audio. To avoid prohibitively expensive manual annotation, we programmatically fuse complementary predictions from specialized weak teachers through time-registered IoU matching. - 2026-10-23, **Melody Guidance for Audio Diffusion Models Without Retraining**, Cristobal Andrade et.al., Paper: [@2610.32211](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32211)
> **Abstract:** Controlling melody in audio generation normally requires training a conditional generative model on audio paired with pitch curves or MIDI notes. We instead guide an existing unconditional diffusion model, originally trained without melody inputs, to follow a target melody without retraining or modifying it. During inference, a differentiable pitch detector steers the sampled audio toward the desired pitches, while the diffusion model keeps it close to the distribution of realistic audio learned during training.
