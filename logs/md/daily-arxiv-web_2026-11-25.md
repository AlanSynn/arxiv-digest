- 2026-11-25, **Motion Beyond Pixels: Learning Future Visual Representations for Object-Centric Dynamics**, Chengzhi Zhao et.al., Paper: [@2611.34644](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34644)
> **Abstract:** Many embodied world models predict future states in latent spaces optimized for RGB reconstruction, which do not explicitly prioritize object-level semantics and geometry relevant to motion generation. We present MotionRep, a visual representation world model that jointly generates future DINO representations and 3D object flow, coupling semantic and geometric information with object-centric dynamics. To make high-dimensional DINO features amenable to generative modeling, we introduce DINO-VAE, which combines temporal and channel compression with latent regularization to produce compact latents while preserving semantic and geometric information. Conditioned on an initial observation, object query points, and a language instruction, a unified diffusion transformer predicts future visual representations and 3D object flow through joint attention. MotionRep employs asymmetric modality-specific noise schedules to keep the visual stream ahead of 3D object flow during joint denoising, allowing progressively refined future visual representations to guide flow generation. Experiments on simulated and real-world manipulation benchmarks demonstrate that MotionRep outperforms pixel-based baselines in 3D object flow accuracy and generalization to unseen scenes, while requiring only three denoising steps for inference. These results support predictive visual representations as an effective future-state space for object-centric dynamics modeling.
- 2026-11-25, **ContextHands: Completing Hand Motion with Learned Context and Joint-Specific Body Kinematics**, Taewoo Kim et.al., Paper: [@2611.34027](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34027)
> **Abstract:** Detailed hand motion contributes to the realism and expressiveness of generated human motion. Completing these details from body motion and language requires connecting the context of an action to the articulation of the hands. We introduce ContextHands, which completes hand motion from an observed body sequence and text descriptions assigned to hands and time intervals. Our approach learns probabilistic hand features from the descriptions and body motion, using auxiliary hand prediction to connect these features to hand articulation. A body encoder preserves joint and body-part features across multiple temporal scales, allowing each hand joint to query these features for joint-specific body information. The diffusion generator combines this joint-specific body information with the learned hand context. Both encoders and the generator are trained jointly, so the final generation objective also shapes the context features. Experiments on the OakInk2 dataset show that ContextHands generates more accurate hand motion and achieves lower Fréchet Hand Distance than existing hand-motion and general human-motion generation methods adapted to the task. In qualitative examples of grasping and tool use, ContextHands more closely reproduces the detailed hand poses in the reference motion. We will release our code and pretrained models to support further research.

> **Abstract:** Foundation models (FMs) have advanced visual understanding and generation by providing semantically rich representations. However, this role of FMs remains underexplored in human motion. Large motion datasets offer diverse movements at scale, but often lack action labels for systematically evaluating learned representations on understanding tasks. Skeleton datasets enable such evaluation through established recognition protocols, yet are typically smaller and focus on predefined actions captured in controlled environments. To combine these strengths, we present MoSk, to the best of our knowledge, the first Motion-Skeleton foundation model to integrate motion and skeleton datasets. - 2026-11-25, **MotionODE: Enhancing Autoregressive Text-to-Motion Generation with Continuous-Depth Modeling**, Zhaoxin Fan et.al., Paper: [@2611.33826](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33826)
> **Abstract:** Autoregressive text-to-motion generation requires local motion predictions to reconcile cross-temporal textual requirements with the available motion context. While existing methods primarily improve motion representations and generation order, the internal formation of conditioned motion representations remains less explored. We introduce MotionODE, a continuous-depth post-training framework that refines hidden-state evolution in pretrained autoregressive generators. MotionODE reinterprets selected Transformer residual branches as local vector fields and replaces their original updates with midpoint Runge--Kutta (RK2) integration, while preserving the motion representation and outer generation mechanism. To address the mismatch between pretrained update paths and newly introduced intermediate states, we propose mixed one-step/two-step RK2 post-training under motion-token supervision, with optional distillation and update-map constraints to preserve pretrained behavior. This minimally invasive framework supports both next-token and next-scale generation without training the generator from scratch. Extensive experiments on HumanML3D demonstrate consistent improvements in text--motion alignment across MoScale, BAMM and LMR, highlighting internal representation updates as a complementary direction for improving autoregressive motion generation.
- 2026-11-25, **MMotion: Collaborative Cross-Scale Memory for Text-to-Motion Generation**, Zhaoxin Fan et.al., Paper: [@2611.33819](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33819)
> **Abstract:** Text-to-motion generation aims to synthesize realistic human motions from natural-language descriptions, enabling applications in animation, virtual avatars, and embodied agents. Despite recent progress, existing methods either retrieve motion exemplars or retain long-range context, but rarely exploit memory to jointly reason about semantics, dynamics, and generation history. In this paper, we propose MMotion, a collaborative multi-scale memory framework that unifies scale-conditioned semantic retrieval, latent dynamics modeling, and causal working memory. Specifically, semantic memory provides motion priors at different generation scales, dynamics memory captures latent state transitions, and working memory preserves causal context from previously generated motions. - 2026-11-24, **MinPair: Measured Minimal Pairs for Fine-Grained Control in Text-to-Motion Generation**, Lennart Schiweck et.al., Paper: [@2611.32549](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32549)
> **Abstract:** Text-to-motion generation has made great progress in capturing what action to perform, yet it remains limited in controlling how that action is executed. For example, when only the height word of a kick request changes, none of the tested state-of-the-art methods reaches all three of knee, waist and head height. The other words of a caption usually imply an attribute such as height, so neither the training loss nor the standard metrics require a model to use the word that states it. A model has to use this word only when it alone separates two otherwise identical captions. We introduce MinPair, which creates such caption pairs from real motion clips. It starts from rule-based motion descriptors that measure the 3D body motion of each clip. From these descriptors a language model writes MinPair captions, timed body-part captions that say how each body part moves. The same descriptors show in which attributes two clips of the same action differ. A measured minimal pair joins the true caption of one clip with an altered copy in which only one attribute takes the value measured in the other clip. A contrastive pair loss then requires a diffusion denoiser to reconstruct the clip more accurately from its true caption than from the altered copy. Training needs no synthetic motion and inference stays unchanged. Control is tested on 22 target tasks and on caption edits that each change one attribute. MinPair captions alone help motions reach their targets and improve the standard metrics M2M, R@3 and FID, yet a model trained on them still ignores most single-word edits. The pair loss raises the share of followed template edits from 11% to 60%, while the standard metrics do not improve further. With body-part captions, MinPair reaches more targets than state-of-the-art methods that were trained on up to 99 times more motion data.
- 2026-11-24, **SetCon: Setup-Conditioned IMU-Language Learning from 3D Human Motion**, Lennart Schiweck et.al., Paper: [@2611.32548](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32548)
> **Abstract:** Aligning inertial measurement unit (IMU) signals with text enables zero-shot activity recognition across wearable devices. A worn device's signal depends on its setup, the combination of body site and mounting. Leading IMU-language models make their encoders invariant to the mounting, which also discards the device's orientation relative to the body, such as the direction of gravity that separates sitting, standing and lying. We introduce SetCon, which replaces full invariance with selective invariance. It estimates each device's mounting on its known body site from a few unlabeled windows and conditions its encoder on this estimate. Both calibration and representation learning are supervised by 3D human motion, since one movement can be simulated under many known sensor setups. At test time, a class is represented by its name or by an anchor, which averages the text embeddings of the name and of activity descriptions and needs no labeled window. A language model built on the frozen encoder also answers how and in what order a movement is performed. Trained only on IMU simulated from the same Nymeria motion capture as AnyMo, SetCon reaches 56.6% mean zero-shot accuracy on 13 real datasets with class names and 65.3% with anchors, against 41.8% and 46.7% for AnyMo.
- 2026-11-24, **COHERE4D: Training-Free Dense Surface Alignment for Coherent Metric 4D Human-Scene Reconstruction**, Zhumei Wang et.al., Paper: [@2611.32482](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32482)
> **Abstract:** Human and scene foundation models offer complementary strengths for monocular 4D reconstruction: human models recover metric-scale body meshes but lack scene grounding, whereas scene models estimate globally consistent 3D geometry and camera motion without reliable metric scale. Crucially, both models reconstruct the same foreground human surface. - 2026-11-24, **Video Models are Excellent Motion Models**, Kian Izadpanah et.al., Paper: [@2611.32432](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32432)
> **Abstract:** Video foundation models have emerged as powerful generators of dynamic visual content, exhibiting remarkable capabilities in synthesizing complex actions and interactions. We argue that these models have also become excellent motion generators, with motion priors that are richer and more general than those learned by dedicated motion models. Their advantage extends beyond visual realism and is manifested in two important aspects: motion naturalness, including coherent body coordination, physical plausibility, and realistic interactions with objects and the environment; and motion scope, encompassing a much broader range of activities and semantic coherence, particularly uncommon, complex, and compound actions. Our findings suggest a shift in the role of specialized motion models, from generating complete animations to guiding and controlling the richer motion capabilities already embedded in video foundation models.
