## Motion Authoring, Editing & Retargeting

- 2026-10-19, **Neural Controlled Directional Frame Generation for Sprite Animation**, Kenneth Alonso et.al., Paper: [@2610.27253](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27253)
> **Abstract:** Sprite-based animation remains prevalent in video game development, but production workflows require capabilities beyond traditional in-betweening. While existing neural interpolation methods can generate intermediate frames, animators also need forward extrapolation to predict motion continuations and backward extrapolation for editing and reverse-engineering existing sprite sheets. Current video generation models either operate on natural video content with stochastic diffusion sampling incompatible with production requirements, or focus exclusively on interpolation without addressing extrapolation tasks. We present a deep learning approach for controlled directional frame generation in sprite animation using three specialized expert models that share identical U-Net encoder-decoder architectures and several domain-specific modifications, including RGBA-aware processing, multi-scale flow refinement, pixel-shuffle upsampling, and alpha-gated blending. Each model is trained on different temporal orderings of the same curated dataset: previous (frames 2,3>1), middle (frames 1,3>2), and next (frames 1,2>3). We demonstrate that only 280 carefully curated sprite triplets enable effective training for all three directional tasks without requiring massive generic datasets. Evaluation on 42 test triplets using the FLIP metric shows that all three models achieve comparable quality--previous (0.26), middle (0.26), and next (0.27)--with no significant degradation for extrapolation versus interpolation, outperforming general-purpose baselines by 17-57%. With deterministic single-pass generation and compact model, our method provides the first practical solution for controllable any-position frame generation specifically tailored for pixel art sprite animation workflows, validating that quality-over-quantity data curation enables specialized deep learning for niche visual domains.

- 2026-10-23, **ViT-H: Generative Video-To-Humanoid Control with Motion Tracking Feedback**, Zimo He et.al., Paper: [@2610.32966](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32966)
> **Abstract:** Learning humanoid control from monocular human videos offers a scalable way to acquire diverse whole-body behaviors. Yet, existing reconstruction-retargeting-tracking pipelines are limited in system-level scalability by separately optimized intermediate stages and cannot directly optimize reference motion executability using downstream tracking feedback. We present ViT-H, a video-to-humanoid control framework following a generation-and-tracking paradigm. ViT-H directly generates humanoid reference motions from videos with a diffusion model, bypassing human motion reconstruction and retargeting, and uses motion-tracking outcomes to post-train the generator with direct preference optimization. We further introduce an autoregressive variant that enables real-time humanoid video teleoperation with sub-400 ms latency. Extensive experiments on the Unitree G1 demonstrate that ViT-H outperforms state-of-the-art reconstruction-retargeting-tracking pipelines in both motion quality and executability. We further show that ViT-H scales favorably with model size and with both pre-training and post-training data scale, while motion-tracking feedback consistently improves executability across different motion trackers.
- 2026-10-23, **Past as State, Present as Attention: Persistent-State Blockwise Flow Matching for Long-Horizon Co-Speech Motion Generation**, Yi Liu et.al., Paper: [@2610.32373](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32373)
> **Abstract:** Co-speech motion generation remains challenging over long horizons due to the need for temporal coherence and consistent style. Existing methods typically generate long motions segment by segment, either conditioning on a short seed of recent frames, which gradually drifts, or retaining ever-longer explicit histories, which are costly to maintain and hard to learn from limited data. We trace these limitations to a key asymmetry between past and present: past motion carries slowly varying style that should be compactly summarized, whereas the current segment exhibits fast-changing local dynamics that require fine-grained modeling. This asymmetry motivates a simple Past-As-State, Present-as-Attention principle for long-horizon co-speech motion generation. Following this principle, we propose PASPA, which summarizes past history with an inter-block persistent state and models the current segment with intra-block bidirectional self-attention. We instantiate PASPA within a blockwise flow matching framework, with multi-block supervision for effective training over variable histories, prefill-decode state caching for efficient rollout, and hybrid classifier-free guidance for enhanced history and speech conditioning. Extensive public-benchmark experiments show that PASPA achieves state-of-the-art FGD performance and preserves motion style across long-sequence generation.

- 2026-10-21, **RLMuscle: Muscle-Driven Character Animation via Reinforcement Learning**, Chunlei Li et.al., Paper: [@2610.30033](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30033)
> **Abstract:** Body deformation caused by muscle contraction plays a vital role in the visual realism of character animation, yet in most 3D animation pipelines artists have to manually specify muscle activations, which is time-consuming and gives no guarantee of physical realism. To address this, we propose a new method in which a reinforcement learning (RL) controller discovers per-muscle activations that track reference motions without manual tuning. Existing RL muscle controllers stop at 1D muscles; we go one step further, keeping the 1D model inside the RL control loop as a fast surrogate, and using a 3D volumetric simulation to produce the final deformation. Because both stages share the very same constitutive law, the muscle deformation is produced by the same activations that generated the motion. We further add a measured electromyography (EMG) prior that steers the redundant activation solution toward real human muscle recruitment. To achieve stable, efficient, and realistic simulation, we propose a novel geometric multigrid for Extended Position-Based Dynamics (XPBD) that is built on a coarse tetrahedral cage with barycentric-coordinate prolongation and closes a V-cycle on tetrahedral meshes. Within this GPU-parallelized solver, we inject the same Hill-type force-length law as an energy-based fiber constraint that turns per-fiber activation into realistic muscle contraction and bulging. We demonstrate volumetric muscle simulation on a 194-muscle human body with a diverse set of motions (walk, kick, swim, overhead-squat, etc.), simulating the full-body model at 18 ms per frame on a single RTX 4090. Our work takes a step toward character animation that is not only visually convincing but also biomechanically grounded. The website with code and videos is at https://chunleili.github.io/project-page-rlmuscle/.

> **Abstract:** Real-world 3D reconstruction pipelines often rely on multi-view images degraded by lossy compression. While compression artifacts have been extensively studied in single-image or video restoration, their impact on multi-view geometry remains largely overlooked. Experiments across diverse datasets, codecs, and compression levels demonstrate gains in restoration quality and downstream 3D tasks, including novel-view synthesis, camera pose estimation, and view matching. These results establish compressed multi-view restoration as a distinct problem and highlight its importance for 3D reconstruction under lossy compression. - 2026-10-22, **OmniRay: Camera-Agnostic Monocular 3D Reconstruction with Composite Ray Fields**, Namitha Guruprasad et.al., Paper: [@2610.31645](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31645)

- 2026-10-22, **HiP3R: Hierarchical Pose Graphs for Long-Horizon 3D Reconstruction**, Zunnan Xu et.al., Paper: [@2610.31635](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31635)
> **Abstract:** Feed-forward models accurately estimate camera motion and 3D geometry from short video windows. However, computational and memory costs prevent direct inference on sequences containing thousands of frames. Long videos must therefore be processed as overlapping chunks. This division produces independent local reconstructions with inconsistent coordinate frames and duplicate estimates of shared cameras. Achieving global consistency requires propagating constraints from adjacent overlaps and distant revisits across the sequence. This requirement naturally motivates pose graph optimization. However, the choice of graph level creates a trade-off. Chunk-level graphs efficiently resolve scale drift and close large loops. Experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of HiP3R in improving camera trajectories and 3D reconstruction. Our method applies to both chunk-based and streaming backends without additional training or fine-tuning. The codes are available at https://kkakkkka.github.io/HiP3R.

> **Abstract:** We address inverse rendering with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by introducing explicit visibility modeling for relighting as well as material and light decomposition. Our approach augments the standard rasterized 3DGS pipeline with a sparse visibility oracle that estimates transmittance-weighted occlusion for Gaussian primitives from sampled lighting directions. This oracle is implemented using a point-based ray tracer accelerated by a bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) and queried sparsely within the inverse-rendering objectives, while the original 3DGS loop is retained for training efficiency, interactive visualization, and tooling compatibility. Shading follows a physically based rendering (PBR) formulation with a principled microfacet bidirectional reflectance distribution function (BRDF) and low-resolution high-dynamic-range (HDR) environment maps. We evaluate our method on the TensoIR synthetic dataset against baselines based on neural radiance fields (NeRFs), tensor representations, and 3DGS. The results show competitive performance and improvements over several earlier baselines for novel-view synthesis (NVS), albedo reconstruction, and relighting. Ablations indicate that explicit visibility modeling is an important contributor to relighting and material-illumination disentanglement, while pure PBR supervision can favor appearance fitting in NVS. Overall, the findings suggest that sparse, point-based visibility queries effectively complement rasterized splatting for inverse rendering while preserving the practical advantages of the 3DGS ecosystem.

## Narrative & Long-form Audio

- 2026-10-23, **Melody Guidance for Audio Diffusion Models Without Retraining**, Cristobal Andrade et.al., Paper: [@2610.32211](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32211)
> **Abstract:** Controlling melody in audio generation normally requires training a conditional generative model on audio paired with pitch curves or MIDI notes. We instead guide an existing unconditional diffusion model, originally trained without melody inputs, to follow a target melody without retraining or modifying it. During inference, a differentiable pitch detector steers the sampled audio toward the desired pitches, while the diffusion model keeps it close to the distribution of realistic audio learned during training. Our main contribution is a sampling method that balances these two directions, providing melody control while preserving audio quality. To improve stability, we initialize sampling with a simple synthesized version of the target melody rather than pure noise. Experiments on piano, guitar, and singing voice show melody accuracy comparable to conditional models trained specifically for melody control. Our method therefore adds melody control to existing audio diffusion models without requiring paired melody data or task-specific retraining.
- 2026-10-16, **Semantic-Critical Regions for Video-to-Audio Generation**, Yun Fang et.al., Paper: [@2610.23021](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23021)
> **Abstract:** In real-world videos, sound events are often associated with localized and fine-grained visual cues, such as object motion, object-part dynamics, contact events, and interactions between objects. Existing video-to-audio (V2A) methods employ implicit conditioning based on global visual representations or explicit control derived from temporal and spatial cues. However, subtle sound-producing and sound-indicative interactions remain insufficiently captured, leaving room for more precise visual conditioning of sound-event semantics and temporal dynamics. Motivated by this observation, we refer to regions containing such sound-relevant visual evidence as Semantic-Critical Regions (SCRs) and introduce SCR-V2A, a V2A generation framework that explicitly localizes and encodes SCRs as fine-grained visual guidance for audio generation. Specifically, we first identify SCRs through sound-related visual localization. We then transform the localized regions into a control representation through adapter-based dual visual encoding, which jointly captures global visual context and fine-grained sound-relevant evidence. The representation is used to condition a downstream audio generation model. Experiments on both in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks demonstrate that SCR-V2A achieves strong performance in distribution matching and audio quality while remaining competitive in cross-modal alignment and requiring few trainable parameters. These results highlight the value of prioritizing fine-grained, sound-relevant visual regions for V2A generation.
