## Motion Authoring, Editing & Retargeting

- 2026-10-12, **Low-Cost Deep Learning for Automated Keyframe Interpolation in Sprite Animation**, Kenneth Alonso et.al., Paper: [@2610.16423](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16423)
> **Abstract:** Sprite-based animation remains prevalent in video game development, but manual in-betweening---generating intermediate frames between keyframes---is labor-intensive. Existing video frame interpolation methods, optimized for photorealistic content, introduce smoothing artifacts when applied to the sharp edges, limited color palettes, and stylized motion characteristic of hand-drawn sprites. We present a deep learning approach for sprite in-betweening that adapts a U-Net encoder-decoder architecture with domain-specific modifications, including RGBA-aware processing, multi-scale flow refinement, and alpha-gated blending to preserve pixel-perfect sprite aesthetics. Following data-centric AI principles, we demonstrate that a few carefully curated sprite animation triplets---selected for diversity, quality, and representative edge cases---enable effective training without requiring massive generic datasets. Experimental evaluation using the FLIP perceptual metric shows that our method achieves a mean error of 0.258 on 42 test triplets, outperforming state-of-the-art video interpolation RIFE (0.313, 17.5\% worse), traditional optical flow Lucas-Kanade (0.449, 42.6\% worse), and general frame synthesis CyclicGen (0.603, 57.2\% worse). This study shows that quality-over-quantity data curation and domain-specific architectural adaptations yield better interpolation quality than general-purpose methods trained on orders of magnitude more data, validating the data-centric AI paradigm for specialized visual domains. This is particularly important for small-to-medium-sized game development studios, where budget and time constraints are critical.
- 2026-10-08, **PriMoRetarget: Humanoid Motion Transfer Through Prioritized Motion Retargeting**, Nicholas Ioannidis et.al., Paper: [@2610.12642](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12642)
> **Abstract:** Motion retargeting is a critical preprocessing step for transferring human motion data to humanoid robots. While existing methods are effective for single-character motions, interactions involving multiple characters and objects introduce additional contacts and spatial relationships that must be preserved. Retargeting is commonly formulated as a weighted multi-objective optimization problem, where conflicting objectives can require careful tuning of relative weights and can still compromise important motion characteristics. We introduce PriMoRetarget, a prioritized motion-retargeting framework that replaces relative objective weighting with an explicit priority hierarchy. Lower-priority updates are constrained to preserve higher-priority objectives, allowing critical characteristics such as foot placement and interaction contacts to take precedence over lower-priority pose objectives. The method is evaluated on four datasets spanning single-character motion, character-object manipulation, and character-character interactions. The resulting motions better preserve task-relevant interactions and provide reference trajectories that can be tracked more reliably than those produced by widely used retargeting methods.

- 2026-10-16, **StopWatch: Goal Grounding for Object-goal Navigation**, Xinhao Liu et.al., Paper: [@2610.23841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23841)
> **Abstract:** Pretrained VLMs bring rich world knowledge and semantic priors to object-goal navigation, and fine-tuning a VLM into a navigation policy has become a common recipe. Despite this progress, success rates remain low, and it is unclear which capability these fine-tuned policies lack. Our diagnosis identifies goal grounding as the dominant failure. These policies explore well, yet often fail to notice that the goal is already in view or to estimate how far away it is. To address this failure, we ground the goal explicitly. Explicit grounding substantially improves success and reaches state-of-the-art results on six benchmarks across three scene collections. We also correct annotation defects in these benchmarks, so that future methods are evaluated more faithfully. Our analysis on the clean benchmarks shows that, with ground-truth grounding, our policy nearly matches the expert that generated its demonstrations, leaving the headroom in perception rather than policy learning. Our work points to goal grounding as a missing piece of object-goal navigation and offers a simple and effective solution to the task.

> **Abstract:** Federated prompt learning enables vision-language models like CLIP to collaboratively adapt lightweight soft prompts across distributed clients without sharing raw data. - 2026-10-16, **Not Every Mention Is a Claim: Rethinking Object Hallucination Detection in Vision-Language Models**, Hsin-Ling Hsu et.al., Paper: [@2610.23610](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23610)
> **Abstract:** Object hallucination in vision-language models (VLMs) is usually measured by checking each object a caption names against the image. Most benchmarks and detectors alike treat every object mention as a claim that the object is present. This assumption fails when a caption names an object without asserting it, as in "a bench for people to sit on", which mentions people without claiming that any are in the image. We call such mentions non-assertive. Treating them as hallucinations inflates the error rates attributed to models that use such language. To separate them from real hallucinations, we introduce NAME-Bench (Non-Assertive Mention Evaluation Benchmark), 25,497 object mentions from captions that five VLMs generate for COCO images. Each mention is labelled concrete (asserted and present), hallucinated (asserted and absent), or non-assertive, and non-assertive mentions are further divided into four subtypes: depictive, purpose, negation and hedged. All the hallucination detectors that we evaluate flag non-assertive mentions as hallucinations, at rates between 14% and 80%. Adding non-assertive mentions to detector training as negatives largely corrects this on LLaVA-1.5. On the four newer VLMs, a comparable correction takes five to ten times more data, and the detectors then catch fewer real hallucinations. These findings show that current detectors conflate mentioning an object with asserting it, and that this conflation is harder to undo on the four newer VLMs than on LLaVA-1.5. Hallucination evaluation should therefore separate what a caption asserts from what it merely mentions.

> **Abstract:** Compact medical vision-language models (VLMs) can answer clinical questions correctly without reliably using the image, relying instead on question wording or learned priors. Standard knowledge distillation copies a teacher's answer preference on the original image, but it leaves unchecked whether that preference is conditional on the visual evidence required by the question. We study this missing target as medical visual evidence dependency: support for the correct answer should decrease when the matched evidence is absent. We introduce Evidence-Conditioned Dependency Distillation (ECDD), which pairs each training image with a control matched to the evidence level and distills the teacher's drop in answer margin across the pair. These results suggest that medical VLM distillation should transfer both the teacher's answer preference and its dependence on visual evidence.

Given an arbitrary shape, our method determines the internal cavity geometry and the number and placement of lights in two stages: an initialization stage fixes the number of lights and establishes their initial positions and the initial cavity geometry, after which gradient-based optimization jointly refines the cavity geometry and light positions via differentiable rendering subject to geometric constraints. We calibrate the material parameters of the translucent shade material prior to optimization to improve the agreement between simulation and the fabricated result. We validate our approach by 3D-printing five lampshade designs of varying geometric complexity and demonstrate that the fabricated lampshades achieve reasonably uniform luminance.

## Narrative & Long-form Audio

- 2026-10-16, **Semantic-Critical Regions for Video-to-Audio Generation**, Yun Fang et.al., Paper: [@2610.23021](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23021)
> **Abstract:** In real-world videos, sound events are often associated with localized and fine-grained visual cues, such as object motion, object-part dynamics, contact events, and interactions between objects. Existing video-to-audio (V2A) methods employ implicit conditioning based on global visual representations or explicit control derived from temporal and spatial cues. However, subtle sound-producing and sound-indicative interactions remain insufficiently captured, leaving room for more precise visual conditioning of sound-event semantics and temporal dynamics. Motivated by this observation, we refer to regions containing such sound-relevant visual evidence as Semantic-Critical Regions (SCRs) and introduce SCR-V2A, a V2A generation framework that explicitly localizes and encodes SCRs as fine-grained visual guidance for audio generation. Specifically, we first identify SCRs through sound-related visual localization. We then transform the localized regions into a control representation through adapter-based dual visual encoding, which jointly captures global visual context and fine-grained sound-relevant evidence. The representation is used to condition a downstream audio generation model. Experiments on both in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks demonstrate that SCR-V2A achieves strong performance in distribution matching and audio quality while remaining competitive in cross-modal alignment and requiring few trainable parameters. These results highlight the value of prioritizing fine-grained, sound-relevant visual regions for V2A generation.
- 2026-10-07, **AutoSynth: Learning to Generate Editable Synthesizer Programs from Audio and Text**, Tristan Wu et.al., Paper: [@2610.10774](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10774)
> **Abstract:** Audio generation models can translate natural-language descriptions into sound, but their outputs are typically waveforms. Their audio quality is constrained by audio compression, and their outputs do not readily support direct edits to notes, timbral parameters, or modulation relationships. We present AutoSynth, which represents MIDI performance events, fixed synthesizer parameters, and variable-length modulation routes as a unified sequence for a synthesizer, and learns their dependencies with an audio-conditioned autoregressive model. A single model supports both tasks. Given reference audio, the model directly predicts a synthesizer program; given text, it uses a pretrained audio generation model and converts the generated audio into a program. Training consists of two stages: supervised learning on large-scale audio-program pairs automatically constructed from a small set of native presets, followed by group-relative policy optimization with a mixed reward combining semantic similarity, pitch-related features, acoustic similarity, and sound usefulness. The pipeline requires neither paired text-target-program annotations nor a differentiable synthesizer. Experiments show that AutoSynth produces complete, editable synthesizer programs and achieves competitive results in both synthesizer inversion and text-driven generation. Audio demos and source code are available at https://auto-synth.github.io/.
