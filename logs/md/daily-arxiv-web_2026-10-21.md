## Motion Authoring, Editing & Retargeting

- 2026-10-19, **Neural Controlled Directional Frame Generation for Sprite Animation**, Kenneth Alonso et.al., Paper: [@2610.27253](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27253)
> **Abstract:** Sprite-based animation remains prevalent in video game development, but production workflows require capabilities beyond traditional in-betweening. While existing neural interpolation methods can generate intermediate frames, animators also need forward extrapolation to predict motion continuations and backward extrapolation for editing and reverse-engineering existing sprite sheets. Current video generation models either operate on natural video content with stochastic diffusion sampling incompatible with production requirements, or focus exclusively on interpolation without addressing extrapolation tasks. We present a deep learning approach for controlled directional frame generation in sprite animation using three specialized expert models that share identical U-Net encoder-decoder architectures and several domain-specific modifications, including RGBA-aware processing, multi-scale flow refinement, pixel-shuffle upsampling, and alpha-gated blending. Each model is trained on different temporal orderings of the same curated dataset: previous (frames 2,3>1), middle (frames 1,3>2), and next (frames 1,2>3). We demonstrate that only 280 carefully curated sprite triplets enable effective training for all three directional tasks without requiring massive generic datasets. Evaluation on 42 test triplets using the FLIP metric shows that all three models achieve comparable quality--previous (0.26), middle (0.26), and next (0.27)--with no significant degradation for extrapolation versus interpolation, outperforming general-purpose baselines by 17-57%. With deterministic single-pass generation and compact model, our method provides the first practical solution for controllable any-position frame generation specifically tailored for pixel art sprite animation workflows, validating that quality-over-quantity data curation enables specialized deep learning for niche visual domains.

## Character Motion Synthesis & Animation

- 2026-10-21, **RLMuscle: Muscle-Driven Character Animation via Reinforcement Learning**, Chunlei Li et.al., Paper: [@2610.30033](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30033)
> **Abstract:** Body deformation caused by muscle contraction plays a vital role in the visual realism of character animation, yet in most 3D animation pipelines artists have to manually specify muscle activations, which is time-consuming and gives no guarantee of physical realism. To address this, we propose a new method in which a reinforcement learning (RL) controller discovers per-muscle activations that track reference motions without manual tuning. Existing RL muscle controllers stop at 1D muscles; we go one step further, keeping the 1D model inside the RL control loop as a fast surrogate, and using a 3D volumetric simulation to produce the final deformation. Because both stages share the very same constitutive law, the muscle deformation is produced by the same activations that generated the motion. We further add a measured electromyography (EMG) prior that steers the redundant activation solution toward real human muscle recruitment. To achieve stable, efficient, and realistic simulation, we propose a novel geometric multigrid for Extended Position-Based Dynamics (XPBD) that is built on a coarse tetrahedral cage with barycentric-coordinate prolongation and closes a V-cycle on tetrahedral meshes. Within this GPU-parallelized solver, we inject the same Hill-type force-length law as an energy-based fiber constraint that turns per-fiber activation into realistic muscle contraction and bulging. We demonstrate volumetric muscle simulation on a 194-muscle human body with a diverse set of motions (walk, kick, swim, overhead-squat, etc.), simulating the full-body model at 18 ms per frame on a single RTX 4090. Our work takes a step toward character animation that is not only visually convincing but also biomechanically grounded. The website with code and videos is at https://chunleili.github.io/project-page-rlmuscle/.
- 2026-10-21, **DewHand: Direct Metric World-Space Hand Motion Reconstruction from Egocentric Videos**, Naru Suzuki et.al., Paper: [@2610.29936](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29936)
> **Abstract:** Recovering metric world-space hand motion from egocentric video supports understanding human interactions and transferring them to embodied agents. Three challenges complicate this task: multi-stage pipelines propagate errors from independently estimated hand and camera motion; monocular observations leave physical scale ambiguous; and occlusion and out-of-view hands admit multiple plausible motion trajectories. We present DewHand, a unified model for direct joint reconstruction of world-space hand and camera motion. To avoid compounding errors from multi-stage estimation, DewHand jointly predicts hand and camera motion in a shared world coordinate system within a single-stage pipeline. To resolve metric-scale ambiguity, it combines scene-level geometric features from VGGT-$Ω$ with metric-space supervision, while hand-specific features preserve fine-grained articulation. To handle occlusion and out-of-view hands, we formulate reconstruction as conditional motion generation with flow matching. Under the same architecture, this generative formulation outperforms direct regression in both accuracy and temporal smoothness with only two sampling steps. Experiments on HOT3D and ARCTIC demonstrate improved world-space accuracy and temporal consistency over state-of-the-art methods. DewHand reduces rigid-aligned world-space error relative to StableHand from 32.9 to 28.4 mm on HOT3D and from 76.2 to 36.2 mm on ARCTIC. It also recovers camera scale within 10% on two thirds of HOT3D clips, versus about a quarter for SLAM. Moreover, in zero-shot evaluations on HoloAssist, H2O, and TACO, captured with different devices, DewHand achieves the lowest rigid-aligned world-space error (WA-MPJPE) on each dataset.
- 2026-10-20, **Learning Adaptable Dexterous Skills via Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking**, Yuxuan Kuang et.al., Paper: [@2610.28628](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28628)
> **Abstract:** Dexterous multi-finger hands hold promise to unlock diverse skills like functional grasps, in-hand reorientation, and dynamic handover that the human-designed world assumes, yet existing learning recipes force a trade-off. Goal-reaching reinforcement learning generalizes to arbitrary object poses but, lacking any human prior, converges to unnatural and unreliable finger strategies; motion tracking reproduces human-like behavior from a single clip but cannot deviate from it. We present AdaDex, a goal-conditioned RL framework that turns a single hand-object trajectory of a few seconds---from mocap data or HOI reconstruction from in-the-wild RGB(D) video---into an adaptable dexterous skill. At the core of AdaDex is Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking: a shared policy is trained on two populations of massively parallel environments: motion tracking environments trained with dense motion tracking objective, which instill the human prior from human motion; and generalization environments with noised initial states and goals and a sparse object-success reward, which force the skill to work far off the reference. From seconds of human motion per skill, the resulting policies can learn diverse adaptable dexterous skills, such as functional grasping, object reposing, in-hand reorientation with finger gaiting, and dynamic behaviors like throw-and-catch. Our method outperforms baselines in terms of generalization and functionality, and we demonstrate transfer across simulators and to the real world. Project page: https://yuxuank.com/AdaDex/.
- 2026-10-20, **VLM-Guided Contact Retargeting and Generative Partner Modeling for Humanoid-Human Interaction**, Yuto Shibata et.al., Paper: [@2610.28523](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28523)
> **Abstract:** Humanoid-human interaction requires robots to operate under severe partial observability: during close physical contact, the human partner is occluded, truncated, or outside the robot's field of view, causing pose estimation to fail and often necessitating external tracking or privileged human state. At the same time, heavy mutual occlusions introduce errors into the human-human motion-capture data used for learning. We present REACT, a framework for learning deployable humanoid-human interaction policies from human-human demonstrations. REACT treats semantic contact structure as the key supervisory signal connecting demonstration processing, interaction learning, and onboard control. First, a VLM-guided contact modeling and retargeting pipeline identifies semantically meaningful contacts and transfers them onto humanoid-human geometry while preserving interaction structure. Using these annotations, privileged interaction policies learn physically grounded behaviors through interaction-aware contact rewards and are then distilled into deployable egocentric depth policies trained entirely from onboard sensing using photorealistic human depth rendering. To address severe partial observability, we introduce generative partner-state modeling objectives that predict partner motion from occluded egocentric views. We evaluate REACT in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid across five contact-rich interactions. Semantic contact modeling substantially improves preservation of demonstrated interactions, while generative partner-state modeling improves contact recall on four of five interactions, most under heavy occlusion. On real hardware, REACT performs all five interactions using onboard sensing alone.

Extensive experiments demonstrate that SMART effectively supports multi-turn conversational motion generation and enables efficient motion refinement.
- 2026-10-20, **Gaze2HOI: Gaze-Aware Intention Disambiguation for Egocentric Hand-Object Interaction Forecasting**, Chuanyang Ma et.al., Paper: [@2610.27877](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27877)
> **Abstract:** Anticipating how users will interact with their surroundings is essential for proactive systems that provide timely assistance in AR, VR, and embodied applications. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Gaze2HOI achieves state-of-the-art performance in both intention inference and motion forecasting, even approaching oracle-level end-to-end performance in specific interaction phases. - 2026-10-20, **World2HOSI: A Multi-Expert Harness for Long-Horizon Human-Object-Scene Interaction Generation**, Jiushu Li et.al., Paper: [@2610.27725](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27725)
> **Abstract:** Long-horizon human-object-scene interaction (HOSI) is central to embodied AI, animation, and game development. - 2026-10-19, **BUMP: Body-driven Unified Multi-object Physics for HOI Synthesis**, Wonjung Woo et.al., Paper: [@2610.27066](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27066)
> **Abstract:** Generating human-object interactions (HOIs) with multiple objects is challenging because the resulting motions arise from different underlying causes. Human motion reflects semantic intent, intentionally manipulated objects must remain coordinated with the human, while surrounding objects move primarily as physical consequences of contact and collision. We present BUMP, a text-driven framework for multi-object HOI generation based on a simple principle: learn intentional motion and simulate physical consequences. Our method first generates full-body human motion from text and scene context, then predicts trajectories for a small number of intentionally manipulated driver objects conditioned on the human motion. Qualitative results showcase diverse scenarios involving inter-object relationships and edits, highlighting the advantage of our unified model formulation.
