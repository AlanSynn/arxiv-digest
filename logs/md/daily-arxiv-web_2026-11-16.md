- 2026-11-16, **DuoFlow: Towards Synchronized Speech and Motion Generation with Fine-Grained Control**, Jun Dong et.al., Paper: [@2611.21530](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21530)
> **Abstract:** An embodied speaker's expressiveness depends not only on what is said, but also on how the voice sounds and how the body moves. However, existing joint speech-motion generation models are largely driven implicitly by transcript semantics or intermediate speech representations, offering little explicit control over motion hierarchy, body parts, or vocal style. To bridge this gap, we construct MotionSpeech, a structured benchmark for controllable joint speech-motion generation that includes transcripts, voice-style descriptions, and motion conditions at two hierarchical levels. Building on this benchmark, we propose DuoFlow, a dual-stream latent flow transformer that jointly denoises speech and motion under a shared noise level and a unified second-based temporal coordinate. Modality-specific residual connectors inject structured conditions into the corresponding streams, layer-wise bidirectional cross-modal attention coordinates their generation, and a monotonic phoneme pathway combines normalized progress anchors with CTC supervision to strengthen speech temporal modeling. DuoFlow provides a unified interface for fine-grained motion and vocal-style control and demonstrates the ability to synthesize high-quality, synchronized, and instruction-consistent speech-motion sequences.
- 2026-11-15, **SGIP: Learning Scene-Grounded Interaction Priors for Scene-Aware Human Motion Prediction**, Donghyeon Soon et.al., Paper: [@2611.20076](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20076)
> **Abstract:** Human motion prediction (HMP) aims to forecast future movement from an observed motion sequence, and recent methods have incorporated 3D scene geometry and object semantics to improve long-term plausibility. However, the destination of future motion remains ambiguous in cluttered scenes: the same observed motion can lead to different interaction targets or navigation modes. Existing methods either handle this ambiguity implicitly or rely on auxiliary signals like gaze that are hard to obtain in practice. To address this, we propose SGIP, which explicitly predicts where in the scene future motion will be grounded across both objects and open navigation regions, and uses it to condition future trajectory and full-body pose prediction. This scene-grounded interaction prior is learned via intent distillation: at training time only, a teacher model scores candidate scene regions by combining an LLM prompted with a textual description of the person's intent with geometric motion-scene features. A student model learns to reproduce these scores from only the observed motion and 3D scene, making intent and LLM unnecessary at test time. Across multiple benchmark datasets, SGIP outperforms prior scene-aware baselines on both trajectory and pose accuracy in a realistic deployment setting where neither gaze nor intent is available at inference, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed framework. The project page is available at https://sgip2026.github.io.
- 2026-11-14, **Spectral Steering: Local Inverse Geometry for Frozen Diffusion Generators**, Angelo Moroncelli et.al., Paper: [@2611.19572](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19572)
> **Abstract:** A frozen diffusion generator may need to change several output properties while preserving others. We study when guidance faithfully realizes such a structured request and whether local inverse correction improves its direction. Our account links this benefit to the interaction between the request and the objective-specific remaining-sampler Jacobian: conditioning alone is insufficient. Spectral steering combines a request-specific cosine-gain predictor with damped inverse correction and fixed-time relinearization for finite edits. Scalar nulls and matched-movement controls test the predicted mechanism. Across 8,923 instances in a retrospectively identified local regime, predicted and realized gains correlate at Spearman 0.79, versus -0.11 for condition number. Experiments span images, audio, robotics, and control and planning, including complex character motion generation. On 50 paired inpainting cases, four corrections improve unobserved-pixel PSNR over Adam by 3.25 dB at equal total derivative traversals. Together, the analysis and controls connect request-dependent geometry to directional gains and establish where these gains translate into downstream performance.
- 2026-11-14, **ImpedanceMimic: Learned Impedance as Action Space for Force-Accurate Motion Imitation**, Matthias Jammot et.al., Paper: [@2611.19153](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19153)
> **Abstract:** Recovering the forces behind a motion is ill-posed: different controllers can reproduce the same movement while generating different contact forces. Physics-based motion imitation resolves this ambiguity through simulation, but relies on fixed-gain proportional-derivative (PD) control. We show that fixed gains impose a trade-off between motion and force tracking, as contact and swing place conflicting demands on joint stiffness and damping. We introduce ImpedanceMimic, a reinforcement-learning policy that modulates per-joint stiffness, damping, and position setpoints from motion and force measurements, but requires only motion at inference. Across 129 participants and six activities, ImpedanceMimic outperforms matched fixed-gain PD under every integration scheme, direct-torque, muscle-actuated and existing imitation controllers in kinematic and force fidelity, approaching a dedicated motion-to-force regressor. By expressing gains as multiples of inertia-scaled priors, a single policy controls subject-specific biomechanical models without explicit anthropometric input and transfers zero-shot to unseen humans and a humanoid robot. The learned impedance forms a low-dimensional representation of motor state, shared across bodies, that generalizes to withheld activities: stiffness scales with load, and damping anticipates unloading.

- 2026-11-08, **Does Pose Encoding Matter? Analyzing Geometric Representations for Discrete Motion Vocabulary Learning**, Amir Kimiyaie et.al., Paper: [@2611.11093](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11093)
> **Abstract:** Compressing continuous human motion into a finite vocabulary of discrete tokens is now the first stage of many motion understanding and generation pipelines. Vector quantization partitions a latent space into Voronoi cells around a finite codebook, but the geometry of that latent space is inherited from the input pose encoding, and it is unclear which encoding admits an efficient discretization. We study four encodings that place skeletal motion in different geometric spaces: Gaussian joint heatmaps on the pixel lattice, which we show to be a morphological dilation of the joint point set by a quadratic structuring function; root-relative joint positions in Euclidean space; local joint rotations on $\SO$ through a continuous 6D embedding; and a redundant full kinematic encoding. For each, we train VQ-VAE tokenizers whose codebooks lie on the unit hypersphere, varying codebook size, temporal stride, and code dimension. Beyond reconstruction, we measure codebook utilization and the effective vocabulary size, i.e., the number of equiprobable spherical Voronoi cells with the same entropy as the observed token distribution. Rotation and position encodings keep converting additional codewords into effective vocabulary, whereas the full kinematic encoding saturates at a few hundred effective cells and heatmaps at fewer than sixty. The smallest code dimension gives the highest utilization for three of the four encodings, and the effect of temporal stride depends on the encoding. Encodings of the same pose family can thus yield effective vocabularies differing by an order of magnitude, a difference that reconstruction loss alone does not reveal, which points to input geometry as a first-order design choice in motion tokenization.

- 2026-11-16, **How VLMs Plan What's Next**, Rohit Gandikota et.al., Paper: [@2611.21600](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21600)
> **Abstract:** When a vision-language model describes an image, it names one object at a time and it rarely repeats itself, yet how the model plans this order is far from obvious. We characterize the mechanism a VLM uses to decide where to look before it looks. The mechanism has four main parts. First, binding heads match the relevant text tokens against the image and resolve them into pointers to the matched image region. Second, the same binding heads bind each written answer to the region it came from, and the model turns it into a progress signal: a cursor, the position in the image up to which the list has been written. Third, a select step chooses the nearest unwritten region after the cursor, or stops. Finally, gaze heads execute the chosen pointer and return a concept of what is drawn there, which language heads and the late MLPs turn into a word in the language of the question. We validate each part of this mechanism through causal patching in Qwen3-VL models on a synthetic visual retrieval task, and test our mechanisms against alternative hypotheses. We further show that the mechanism holds across input formats, natural photographs, and languages, and that its binding, relay, and gaze heads appear at every model size from 2B to 32B. This work allows us to investigate how a VLM turns a question and an image into a sequence of answers, layer by layer and token by token. More broadly, it opens a way to study how models plan over complex multi-step tasks with long rollouts. Our project is available at https://plan.baulab.info/
