- 2026-11-18, **Probing Physics in Motion Diffusion Models Against Their Own Estimate**, Saeed Ghorbani et.al., Paper: [@2611.24423](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24423)
> **Abstract:** Recent studies report that video diffusion models "know more than they show": physical properties decode better from their activations than from their inputs or outputs. But a diffusion model computes its clean-sample estimate from those same activations, so such a comparison measures how easily a readout extracts the information. Whether the model hides anything is a separate question. We set out to find hidden physical knowledge in kinematic text-to-motion diffusion models, where the physics of every intermediate estimate can be computed exactly, and recorded every layer along the sampling trajectories of MDM (two checkpoints) and MotionDiffuse. Against the usual baselines the layers do look ahead of the model's own estimate on balance and flight. With every readout tuned the same way, the estimate closes the gap: for balance and flight, adding a layer gains at most 0.01, a size that a positive control shows we would detect. The fall rate of jumps is the one clear exception. Early in denoising, MDM's layers add about 0.08 in $R^2$ (up to 0.11 in a refit) that the estimate does not yet show, and the gain fades as sampling proceeds; on MotionDiffuse it is small and not robust. The estimate also flags floating samples without training, but aborting them saves under a tenth of the compute. In a video model, plausibility that linear probes miss in Wan 2.1's clean latent becomes readable after one fixed nonlinearity.
- 2026-11-18, **SemPhys: Semantic-Physical Co-Learning for Humanoid Motion Generation via Differentiable World Models**, Zejian Kang et.al., Paper: [@2611.24074](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24074)
> **Abstract:** In human-robot collaboration, natural language lets people direct a humanoid's whole-body movements without programming joint trajectories. A common approach generates a kinematic reference from an instruction and relies on a tracking controller to execute it. However, as the controller maintains balance under contact constraints, the executed motion can depart from the reference in ways that change the action it conveys. Our central insight is that semantic alignment must be evaluated directly on the complete motion the robot physically executes. Building on this insight, we introduce \textbf{SemPhys}, a framework that trains motion generators using semantic feedback from simulated executions. A differentiable world model and a fixed tracking controller predict a short segment of a recorded execution. SemPhys inserts this prediction at its original position, scores the complete trajectory against the instruction, and backpropagates through the predicted segment. A paired anchoring loss stabilizes adaptation by keeping each generated reference close to its counterpart from the initial generator. On BONES-SEED, SemPhys largely preserves instruction retrieval accuracy from reference to execution and achieves the highest retrieval accuracy on executed motions among the evaluated methods.
- 2026-11-17, **Wear and Go: Calibration Free Sparse Inertial Motion Capture via Online Sensor Setup Estimation**, Yuanshuo Tan et.al., Paper: [@2611.22894](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22894)
> **Abstract:** Sparse inertial motion capture typically assumes that each sensor's anatomical identity and mounting orientation are known. We present MOSAIC (Motion-based Sensor Assignment and Inertial Calibration), a framework that recovers this setup from ordinary motion without a calibration pose. Our central insight is that body pose changes continuously while the sensor setup persists, allowing movements that are individually ambiguous to provide complementary evidence over time. Transfer across pose networks supports setup recovery as a reusable interface between interchangeable sensors and pretrained pose models.
- 2026-11-17, **ForceBody: Force-Paired Parametric Body Motion with Torque Uncertainty**, Joonwoo Kwon et.al., Paper: [@2611.22769](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22769)
> **Abstract:** Forces and torques drive human motion, central to biomechanics, robotics, and physics-aware animation, yet they are often absent from learning-based motion pipelines for body reconstruction and generation. Some motion datasets provide force annotations, but the paired motion sequences are incompatible with the parametric body models used in modern learning pipelines. In addition, existing datasets typically obtain joint torques through inverse dynamics, which is inherently sensitive to the quality of the input kinematics; however, they do not quantify the reliability of these torque estimates. We benchmark six architectures (MLP, Conv1D, LSTM, GRU, Mamba2, Transformer) on ground reaction force and joint torque prediction from SKEL motion. As one example use of the released uncertainty, finetuning a Transformer with per-sample uncertainty weighting reduces the MAE of joint torque estimation by 5%. Code and data are at https://github.com/DominoAI-Lab/ForceBody-NeurIPS-ED-2026.

> **Abstract:** Human motion prediction (HMP) aims to forecast future movement from an observed motion sequence, and recent methods have incorporated 3D scene geometry and object semantics to improve long-term plausibility. > **Abstract:** A frozen diffusion generator may need to change several output properties while preserving others. Together, the analysis and controls connect request-dependent geometry to directional gains and establish where these gains translate into downstream performance.

> **Abstract:** Recovering the forces behind a motion is ill-posed: different controllers can reproduce the same movement while generating different contact forces. - 2026-11-08, **Does Pose Encoding Matter? Analyzing Geometric Representations for Discrete Motion Vocabulary Learning**, Amir Kimiyaie et.al., Paper: [@2611.11093](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11093)
> **Abstract:** Compressing continuous human motion into a finite vocabulary of discrete tokens is now the first stage of many motion understanding and generation pipelines. Vector quantization partitions a latent space into Voronoi cells around a finite codebook, but the geometry of that latent space is inherited from the input pose encoding, and it is unclear which encoding admits an efficient discretization. We study four encodings that place skeletal motion in different geometric spaces: Gaussian joint heatmaps on the pixel lattice, which we show to be a morphological dilation of the joint point set by a quadratic structuring function; root-relative joint positions in Euclidean space; local joint rotations on $\SO$ through a continuous 6D embedding; and a redundant full kinematic encoding. Encodings of the same pose family can thus yield effective vocabularies differing by an order of magnitude, a difference that reconstruction loss alone does not reveal, which points to input geometry as a first-order design choice in motion tokenization.
