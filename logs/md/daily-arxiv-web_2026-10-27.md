- 2026-10-23, **ViT-H: Generative Video-To-Humanoid Control with Motion Tracking Feedback**, Zimo He et.al., Paper: [@2610.32966](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32966)
> **Abstract:** Learning humanoid control from monocular human videos offers a scalable way to acquire diverse whole-body behaviors. Yet, existing reconstruction-retargeting-tracking pipelines are limited in system-level scalability by separately optimized intermediate stages and cannot directly optimize reference motion executability using downstream tracking feedback. We present ViT-H, a video-to-humanoid control framework following a generation-and-tracking paradigm. ViT-H directly generates humanoid reference motions from videos with a diffusion model, bypassing human motion reconstruction and retargeting, and uses motion-tracking outcomes to post-train the generator with direct preference optimization. We further introduce an autoregressive variant that enables real-time humanoid video teleoperation with sub-400 ms latency. Extensive experiments on the Unitree G1 demonstrate that ViT-H outperforms state-of-the-art reconstruction-retargeting-tracking pipelines in both motion quality and executability. We further show that ViT-H scales favorably with model size and with both pre-training and post-training data scale, while motion-tracking feedback consistently improves executability across different motion trackers.
- 2026-10-23, **Past as State, Present as Attention: Persistent-State Blockwise Flow Matching for Long-Horizon Co-Speech Motion Generation**, Yi Liu et.al., Paper: [@2610.32373](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32373)
> **Abstract:** Co-speech motion generation remains challenging over long horizons due to the need for temporal coherence and consistent style. Existing methods typically generate long motions segment by segment, either conditioning on a short seed of recent frames, which gradually drifts, or retaining ever-longer explicit histories, which are costly to maintain and hard to learn from limited data. We trace these limitations to a key asymmetry between past and present: past motion carries slowly varying style that should be compactly summarized, whereas the current segment exhibits fast-changing local dynamics that require fine-grained modeling. This asymmetry motivates a simple Past-As-State, Present-as-Attention principle for long-horizon co-speech motion generation. Following this principle, we propose PASPA, which summarizes past history with an inter-block persistent state and models the current segment with intra-block bidirectional self-attention. We instantiate PASPA within a blockwise flow matching framework, with multi-block supervision for effective training over variable histories, prefill-decode state caching for efficient rollout, and hybrid classifier-free guidance for enhanced history and speech conditioning. Extensive public-benchmark experiments show that PASPA achieves state-of-the-art FGD performance and preserves motion style across long-sequence generation.

> **Abstract:** Synthesizing physically grounded dexterous hand-object interactions is important for VR/AR, gaming, and robotics, yet remains challenging because subtle contact dynamics, diverse object geometries, and target trajectories must be jointly respected while maintaining natural grasps. - 2026-10-21, **RLMuscle: Muscle-Driven Character Animation via Reinforcement Learning**, Chunlei Li et.al., Paper: [@2610.30033](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30033)
> **Abstract:** Body deformation caused by muscle contraction plays a vital role in the visual realism of character animation, yet in most 3D animation pipelines artists have to manually specify muscle activations, which is time-consuming and gives no guarantee of physical realism. To address this, we propose a new method in which a reinforcement learning (RL) controller discovers per-muscle activations that track reference motions without manual tuning. Existing RL muscle controllers stop at 1D muscles; we go one step further, keeping the 1D model inside the RL control loop as a fast surrogate, and using a 3D volumetric simulation to produce the final deformation. Because both stages share the very same constitutive law, the muscle deformation is produced by the same activations that generated the motion. We further add a measured electromyography (EMG) prior that steers the redundant activation solution toward real human muscle recruitment. Within this GPU-parallelized solver, we inject the same Hill-type force-length law as an energy-based fiber constraint that turns per-fiber activation into realistic muscle contraction and bulging. We demonstrate volumetric muscle simulation on a 194-muscle human body with a diverse set of motions (walk, kick, swim, overhead-squat, etc.), simulating the full-body model at 18 ms per frame on a single RTX 4090. Our work takes a step toward character animation that is not only visually convincing but also biomechanically grounded. The website with code and videos is at https://chunleili.github.io/project-page-rlmuscle/.

> **Abstract:** Recovering metric world-space hand motion from egocentric video supports understanding human interactions and transferring them to embodied agents. We present DewHand, a unified model for direct joint reconstruction of world-space hand and camera motion. DewHand reduces rigid-aligned world-space error relative to StableHand from 32.9 to 28.4 mm on HOT3D and from 76.2 to 36.2 mm on ARCTIC. It also recovers camera scale within 10% on two thirds of HOT3D clips, versus about a quarter for SLAM. Moreover, in zero-shot evaluations on HoloAssist, H2O, and TACO, captured with different devices, DewHand achieves the lowest rigid-aligned world-space error (WA-MPJPE) on each dataset.

> **Abstract:** Dexterous multi-finger hands hold promise to unlock diverse skills like functional grasps, in-hand reorientation, and dynamic handover that the human-designed world assumes, yet existing learning recipes force a trade-off. We further extend TAC2 with expressive speech attributes to obtain TAC2-S, using parameter-efficient specialists to elicit latent paralinguistic capabilities from the pre-trained backbone and distill them into a unified model. We validate the resulting representation in two complementary settings. Paired with frozen text-only LLMs, it yields strong spoken audio understanding across diverse reasoning backends, demonstrating the utility of decoupling fine-grained acoustic perception from downstream reasoning. Used as paired supervision for text-to-audio generation, it supports semantically and temporally grounded reconstruction, together with explicit zero-shot control over event timing, speaker attributes, and word-level emphasis.
- 2026-10-23, **Melody Guidance for Audio Diffusion Models Without Retraining**, Cristobal Andrade et.al., Paper: [@2610.32211](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32211)
> **Abstract:** Controlling melody in audio generation normally requires training a conditional generative model on audio paired with pitch curves or MIDI notes. We instead guide an existing unconditional diffusion model, originally trained without melody inputs, to follow a target melody without retraining or modifying it. During inference, a differentiable pitch detector steers the sampled audio toward the desired pitches, while the diffusion model keeps it close to the distribution of realistic audio learned during training. Our main contribution is a sampling method that balances these two directions, providing melody control while preserving audio quality. To improve stability, we initialize sampling with a simple synthesized version of the target melody rather than pure noise. Experiments on piano, guitar, and singing voice show melody accuracy comparable to conditional models trained specifically for melody control. Our method therefore adds melody control to existing audio diffusion models without requiring paired melody data or task-specific retraining.
- 2026-10-16, **Semantic-Critical Regions for Video-to-Audio Generation**, Yun Fang et.al., Paper: [@2610.23021](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23021)
> **Abstract:** In real-world videos, sound events are often associated with localized and fine-grained visual cues, such as object motion, object-part dynamics, contact events, and interactions between objects. Existing video-to-audio (V2A) methods employ implicit conditioning based on global visual representations or explicit control derived from temporal and spatial cues. However, subtle sound-producing and sound-indicative interactions remain insufficiently captured, leaving room for more precise visual conditioning of sound-event semantics and temporal dynamics. Motivated by this observation, we refer to regions containing such sound-relevant visual evidence as Semantic-Critical Regions (SCRs) and introduce SCR-V2A, a V2A generation framework that explicitly localizes and encodes SCRs as fine-grained visual guidance for audio generation. Specifically, we first identify SCRs through sound-related visual localization. We then transform the localized regions into a control representation through adapter-based dual visual encoding, which jointly captures global visual context and fine-grained sound-relevant evidence. The representation is used to condition a downstream audio generation model. Experiments on both in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks demonstrate that SCR-V2A achieves strong performance in distribution matching and audio quality while remaining competitive in cross-modal alignment and requiring few trainable parameters. These results highlight the value of prioritizing fine-grained, sound-relevant visual regions for V2A generation.
